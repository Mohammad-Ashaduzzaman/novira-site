The problem

Most tracking tells you where something is. Almost none of it can be trusted later.

Visibility isn't the same as proof

A dot on a map answers "where is it right now." But when something goes wrong and the question becomes "what actually happened, and can you prove it" — most tracking data falls apart. It was captured to be seen, not to be relied on.

You can't verify what wasn't captured to be verifiable

Everything downstream — the verification, the proof, the protection — depends on what was recorded at the source. If the first observation was sloppy, untethered, or easy to alter, no amount of processing later can make it trustworthy.

Why this matters

Trust can't be added later. It has to be there at the source.

You can verify, seal, share, and act on an observation — but only if the observation was worth trusting in the first place. That's why Track isn't an afterthought bolted onto a tracker. It's built to capture for verifiability from the first instant: tied to a known device, a known asset, a known moment — so that everything Novira does next has solid ground to stand on. Get the first word right, and the whole record holds.
