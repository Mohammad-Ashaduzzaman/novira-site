The problem

The moment you send a copy, the truth starts to drift.

Everyone ends up with their own version

A PDF emailed here, a screenshot forwarded there, a spreadsheet exported and edited. Within days, each party holds a slightly different account — and no one can say which is the real one. You've shared the file, but not the truth.

And a copy can always be questioned

Was this the original? Was it changed before it reached me? A forwarded document carries no proof of its own integrity — so the moment it matters, it's back to doubt, and back to the argument you were trying to settle.

Why this is hard

Sharing a file is easy. Sharing trust is not.

Anyone can send a document. The hard part is that everyone who receives it can still doubt it — because a copy is just a copy, severed from whatever made the original trustworthy. To truly share truth, every party has to see the same record, know it's the real one, and know it hasn't been touched. That's not a sharing problem. It's a trust problem. NovaLink solves the trust problem.
