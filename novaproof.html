The problem

When goods are damaged, delayed, or lost, the argument that follows is rarely about what to do. It's about what happened.

Everyone is guessing

The shipper believes the cargo left in perfect condition. The carrier believes it was handled correctly. The receiver sees damage. The insurer trusts no one. Each holds a piece. None holds proof.

And the cost is real

Disputes drag for weeks. Claims stall waiting for evidence that doesn't exist. Relationships fray. The loss isn't only the damaged goods — it's the time, the doubt, and the deals that don't survive the argument.

Why this is hard

Data isn't the problem. Trusted data is.

Sensors produce readings. Logs fill up. Dashboards glow. But a number on a screen proves nothing when money is on the line — because anyone can question where it came from, whether it was changed, and whether it really describes this shipment, at this moment. Raw data is an opinion with a timestamp. Evidence is something else entirely.
